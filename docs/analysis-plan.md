## The analysis plan for Billet and its twin (2026-11-01)

**Primary readout.** Δ = held-out bits per byte (bpb) of MetalForge Billet minus its twin's (the same run with the table off): final checkpoints (step 24,415), raw weights, scored in float32 on the 7,314 held-out documents of store `9545f2d205efd574`. Below zero, Billet's bpb is lower.

**Bar.** δ = max(0.01 nats per token, 4σ), the bar in MetalForge's architecture spec, recorded on 13 Oct (commit 5857b9db). Here 0.01 nats per token is 0.0032 bpb (0.01 × 7,636,111 targets ÷ 34,765,120 bytes ÷ ln 2). σ, fixed now, is 0.00108 bpb: the root mean square of the ten three-seed validation SDs at 135M parameters in MoME's Tables 2 and 17 (arXiv:2609.15126; no memory, value embeddings, eight bigram tables). So δ = 4σ = 0.00432 bpb; Δ is compared with it before rounding. One seed per arm cannot run the spec's one-sided Welch test, so Δ is read against δ directly.

**Outcomes.** Δ ≤ −δ: the table helps. Δ ≥ +δ: it hurts. In between: no measurable difference. Two independent runs differ by about σ√2 = 0.00153 bpb (1 SD), so equivalence (no difference larger than δ) is claimed if, and only if, |Δ| < 0.0018 bpb, where a 90% interval for seed noise, Δ ± 1.645 × 0.00153 bpb, lies inside ±δ. One pair detects a true 0.0056 bpb 4 times in 5; smaller effects often read as no measurable difference. With no true difference, a pair falls outside ±δ about 1 time in 200.

**Secondary readouts.**
- Δ with EMA weights, read with the same bands.
- Δ at both arms' pinned step-19,532 checkpoints (where the decay starts), raw weights.
- Five decontaminated zero-shot tasks (PIQA, HellaSwag, ARC-Easy, LAMBADA, Winogrande), raw weights: McNemar's exact two-sided test on each task's headline accuracy, Holm-corrected across the five at a family-wise 5%, and Δ in the gold answer's bpb.
- Training loss, Billet − twin per step on the same batch (nats per token), averaged over 1,000-step windows ending at 10, 25, 50, 80 and 100% of training; steps whose batch hashes differ are left out and counted.
- Memorisation: Δ, raw weights, on about 7,314 training documents, a third each from early, middle and late training (fixed seed, trained part only), beside held-out Δ, with each arm's training − held-out gap by third; run only if its code is in the analysis code and scorer commit recorded before any Δ; reported whatever it shows, or as not run.
- Per Mac-hour: r, the table's step-time cost, follows the rule posted here on 31 Oct (https://github.com/cogvera-labs/MetalForge/issues/235#issuecomment-6543579907). k(s) = s′/s, where s′ is the first step at which the twin's training loss reaches Billet's at step s, both smoothed by a centred 1,001-step moving mean, for s and s′ from 1,500 to 19,031, so every smoothing window lies in the hold (steps 1,000 to 19,531, between the warm-up and the decay); an s with no such s′ is left out and counted; k is the median of k(s). The table leads per Mac-hour in the hold if k is above r's 95% interval, trails if below it, and otherwise neither.

**Intervals.** Every Billet − twin figure except k gets a 95% percentile interval from 10,000 paired bootstrap resamples with a fixed seed: of documents, of zero-shot items, or of 50-step moving blocks of steps. They measure evaluation noise only and are labelled so. Seed noise enters only through σ, in the rules above for raw and EMA Δ; any seed spread measured later, such as PolyPythias-160M's on this text or a second seed at this size, is reported beside σ and does not change δ. A zero-shot difference is claimed only by its Holm-corrected test; the step-19,532, training-loss, memorisation and split figures are described, not tested.

**Splits** of the primary Δ, pre-specified: (1) deciles of targets by the summed training read count of the bigram and trigram rows read at the position predicting them (Billet's final `table/reads.npy`); (2) each document's first window only; (3) documents with and without a 13-gram in the store's training split, the former also by quartile of overlap share (the share of their distinct 13-grams found there), set from the scan alone. Any analysis not named here is exploratory and labelled so.

**The twin's learning-rate sweep** (at 8fd42291) repeats Billet's sweep of 22–23 Oct (item 7 of https://github.com/cogvera-labs/MetalForge/issues/235#issuecomment-6336783798), run at 2bf5f48a, which has the same training code, lock file and Python version as 8fd42291. Each run, as in Billet's: 50M tokens (382 steps of 131,072 tokens), a 157-step warm-up set by hand (the warm-up rule's 1,000-step floor does not fit) and a 77-step decay to zero. The twin runs at peak rates 1e-3, 3e-3, 1e-2 and 3e-2; Billet adds 3e-2. Billet's three earlier points are reused if a 20-step replay of its 1e-2 run at 8fd42291 matches the record bit for bit (loss and batch hash); otherwise they are rerun. A run's score is its mean training loss over its last 38 steps; a non-finite loss or a skipped step there rules it out. Each arm's best rate is the lowest rate scoring within 1% of that arm's lowest score. The pair stays at 1e-2 whatever wins. If the twin's best rate is neither 1e-2 nor Billet's best, and its score at 1e-2 is more than 1% above its lowest score, the sweep's trigger fires, and both twins are reported. The twin's sweep losses are read only after the twin ends.

**The next two runs at Billet's scale** are chosen by this table at the Mon 16 Nov readout and confirmed on Fri 4 Dec:

| Reading | First run | Second run |
|---|---|---|
| The sweep's trigger fired | twin at its best rate | first run of the row Δ falls in |
| Δ ≤ −0.005 bpb | table at 1/16 size (0.375B parameters), seed 0 | twin, seed 1 |
| −0.005 bpb < Δ < +δ | twin, seed 1 | Billet, seed 1 |
| Δ ≥ +δ | Billet cooled down from step 19,532 with the table's rate annealed to zero, then twin, seed 1 | Billet, seed 1 |

**No interim looks.** From this record until the twin's last step, it is watched only through lines that show no loss: its status (phase, step, time left) and, if a start is refused, the guard's reason. Every Billet − twin readout is computed only after the twin ends.

**Amendments.** Before any Δ is computed, a dated amendment here records the analysis code's SHA-256 and the scorer commit used for every model. Later changes are made only by dated amendments saying what changed, why, and whether any comparison of Billet with the twin had been seen. This comment is never edited.

**State at posting.** No one has computed Billet minus the twin on any readout above. Before the twin started, setup checks on 29 Oct ran its command for at most 20 steps in scratch folders, setting each step's loss and step time beside Billet's, and a timing-probe smoke test gave a ratio that is not a measurement. The timing probe itself ran on 1 Nov, between Billet's end and the twin's start; no one has viewed its output. On 1 Nov, after the twin's first night, the maintainer opened the training dashboard, which showed the twin at step 1,312 of 24,415 with a recent mean training loss of 3.601 nats per token; it has not been compared with Billet's. No one has opened the twin's log, metrics or checkpoints, except one agent session that read the last five lines of its log at about 09:35 AWST (save and stop lines only) and later learned the dashboard figure from the maintainer.

**Decisions since M1**, taken by the maintainer on 1 Nov at about 20:35 AWST and on 2 Nov, when no comparison of Billet with the twin had been seen beyond those the State line below names:
1. **A split bin of one document has no interval.** M1 gives every Billet − twin figure except k a 95% interval. A split bin whose nats and bytes lie in one document now has none, and its label says why ("one document"): every resample that draws the document gives the bin the same bpb. In any other bin, a resample that draws none of the bin's bytes gives the bin no bpb, so that resample is left out of the bin's interval and counted, as M1 does for an s with no s′.
2. **Both zero-shot records are held to sequence length 2,048.** M1 names no sequence length. Billet's and the twin's zero-shot records must both be at 2,048, as the analysis code already requires of their held-out records; a record at any other length, or with none, is refused. The arms are scored on different days, and 2,048 is the length both trained at, so this keeps the zero-shot comparison like for like.
3. **k(s) keeps its reading at the start of the range.** If the twin's smoothed loss at s′ = 1,500 is already at or below Billet's at s, the range cannot show the step at which the twin got there, so s has no such s′: it is left out and counted, separately from an s whose loss the twin does not reach by step 19,031. Taking s′ = 1,500 instead would date a crossing that came before the range at its first step. The maintainer confirmed this reading of M1; its text is unchanged.
4. **The analysis code's edits stand.** Claude Code wrote the code in the maintainer's local session, where `harness status` read `NOT ARMED`. The repository's rule (`AGENTS.md`) is that such a session makes no edits; the maintainer accepted these, as #484's body discloses. `overlap.py` (#485) was written the same way, as its body discloses; the maintainer accepted it on 2 Nov.

## The timing probe's analysis rule, recorded before it runs (2026-10-31)

The paired timing probe measures the table's step-time cost. It runs once Billet finishes, early on Sun 1 Nov (AWST), and has not run yet. Its result will be read with the rule its code already applies, and no other:

- After an unused 5-minute warm-up block, 16 blocks of 6 minutes run in the order ABBABAABBAABABBA (A: table on, B: table off). Each block's first 3 steps are dropped.
- Each pair of neighbouring blocks gives r = mean step time with the table on ÷ mean step time with it off.
- The estimate averages log r over the pairs that ran the table first and over those that ran it second, then averages the two (the 2×2 crossover estimate). Its 95% interval is a t-interval on 6 degrees of freedom.
- A pair is left out if either of its blocks saw swap use rise, memory pressure above normal or battery power.

SHA-256 of the code and its description at 20:50 AWST on 31 Oct:
- `local-tools/probe/onoff.py`: `d55caf7625084f1c5c6977a704c18c3bb71e81772c3d659f3a702d5214cce43b`
- `twin/probe/PROBE.md`: `a1ac7ac36217bba3b3a4af8d49b01cbe2fa213f9df5aa30188162a29e559bc9c`

Neither file is in the repository. Anyone given them can check them against these hashes.

The analysis plan for comparing Billet with its twin follows here by Mon 2 Nov, before anyone compares them.

### 7. Learning-rate sweep: three rates, about 50M tokens each

**Runnable on main 979c04c** (the cache cap, #401 and (d2d) are in). Eager attention: no `attn.path` flag.

Run this at the `--accum` that item 6 chose. The commands below assume ×32.

```sh
for LR in 1e-3 3e-3 1e-2; do
  FRC --data "$MF_DATA" --shape poc-150m --table params=6e9 \
     --rows 2 --seq-len 2048 --accum 32 --tokens 5e7 --recipe lr.peak=$LR \
     --print-every 20 --ckpt-every 0 --out ~/mf-runs/lr-sweep-$LR
done
```

- **Size.** At ×32 each run is about 381 optimiser steps. At about 7–9k tok/s each run takes about 1.5–2 h, or about 5–6 h for all three (ESTIMATE).
- **Which rate the table follows.** `lr.peak` also sets the embedding rate unless `lr.emb` is given, and the table's rate is `lr_mult` × the embedding rate (`table/config.py`), so the table's rate moves with the sweep.
- **How to pick.** Take the rate with the lowest mean loss over the last 10% of steps. If two rates finish within 1% of each other, take the lower one.
- **If a run is interrupted.** Checkpoints are off, so delete its `--out` and run it again.
